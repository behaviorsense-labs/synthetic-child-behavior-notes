# Dataset Categories

This file lists the planned categories for the synthetic child behavior notes dataset.

The categories are simple situation labels. They are not diagnoses or clinical labels.

## Categories for v1

| Category | Simple Meaning | Example Situation |
|---|---|---|
| `screen-time ending` | Difficulty when screens are stopped | Tablet time ends and the child cries |
| `transition difficulty` | Difficulty moving from one activity/place to another | Leaving playground or changing activity |
| `homework frustration` | Frustration during schoolwork or learning tasks | Refusing writing, math, or reading |
| `bedtime resistance` | Difficulty with bedtime routine | Refusing pajamas, brushing teeth, or going to bed |
| `mealtime refusal` | Difficulty around eating or sitting for meals | Refusing dinner or leaving table |
| `sensory overload` | Distress around noise, crowds, lights, textures, or smells | Covering ears in a loud store |
| `communication frustration` | Frustration when the child cannot express needs clearly | Crying or yelling when not understood |
| `social conflict` | Conflict with another child or peer | Arguing over toys or sharing |
| `classroom attention` | Difficulty staying focused in classroom-like tasks | Leaving seat or not following group direction |
| `waiting / turn-taking` | Difficulty waiting or taking turns | Upset when waiting in line or losing a game |
| `cleanup difficulty` | Difficulty stopping play and cleaning up | Refusing to put toys away |
| `morning routine` | Difficulty during morning tasks | Refusing clothes, shoes, breakfast, or leaving home |
| `public place overload` | Distress in busy public settings | Crying in a mall, store, airport, or event |
| `separation anxiety` | Distress when separating from caregiver | Crying at school drop-off |
| `routine change` | Difficulty when normal routine changes | Upset because plans changed unexpectedly |

## Notes

These categories are meant to organize synthetic examples for AI and NLP experiments.

They should not be used to label, diagnose, or judge a child.
